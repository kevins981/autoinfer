## partner (20:45:24)

I’d first challenge (B)’s jump from “DRAM is idle 35–45% of the time” to “we could reduce the step to 16–17 ms.”

That assumes idle bandwidth can fetch weights sufficiently far ahead **and keep them resident until use**. Your 72 MB L2 holds only about 72 µs of weight reads at the stated bandwidth. Repeated short prefetch opportunities could help, but aggregate DRAM utilization doesn’t establish how much time you can recover. The 16–17 ms estimate is currently an assumption.

**For one representative idle interval outside the already-prefetched all-reduce windows, how many bytes of upcoming weights can be loaded and retained until their consuming GEMM?**

I’m asking because that quantity determines whether the unused bandwidth is actually usable by a weight pump.

I’d challenge “(A) is the only lever” through your dismissal of lossless compression.

Your entropy estimate gives an ideal weight size of \(27 \times 6.3/8 \approx 21.3\) GB. That doesn’t establish a practical representation or enough room for runtime state. So “decompression is too expensive” is load-bearing beyond the bandwidth savings you considered.
