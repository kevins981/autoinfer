# T32-2 (leader-32): own web research beyond deck T32-1 — candidates for THIS system

CPU only, no GPU jobs. Bar for reference: payoff-if-it-works >= +5% over T31-003 (1088.9 -> 1143.3).

## Where the time goes (the condition every candidate is checked against)
- Wall split: decode steps ~60%, mixed (prefill-carrying) steps ~40% (KB77, KB30/31).
- Decode step (T29-006 node nsys, my re-analysis agents/leader-32/seed/dec.py; T31-003 is ~1 ms less GEMM): 24.9 ms span, busy 24.83 ms (GPU never idle), ~1034 kernels. GEMMs 13.2 ms (264 calls, near the HBM/warm floors, KB79/KB98); host-staged AR + L2 prefetch 6.9 ms (137 calls); GDN replay 1.7; lm_heads ~1.0; small kernels < 8 us 1.46 ms in total.
- Mixed steps: FP8 GEMMs at cuBLASLt speed minus mandatory per-128-K promotion (KB91). The AR is fully exposed, ~1/3 of big steps, 55-65% of it after the mixer out_proj (T30-002, KB100). The AR-off floor: decode -12%, big mixed -31%.
- Fixed facts: no GPU P2P (PCIe through the host, ~21-26 GB/s per direction); GPU0 clock locked at 2235 MHz (KB8); ~1-1.5 GiB/rank memory slack (T31-003 official server.log); MTP k=2, acceptance 2.33 (a1 0.78, a2 0.56, KB60).
- **The only bucket that is both large and not at a floor is inter-GPU communication (~15-17% of wall).** The rules fix its precision (communication stays as baseline; lossless coding is fine) and the hardware fixes its link.

## What I searched (web, 2026-11-10)
Stage-specific parallelism for PCIe GPUs (Seesaw); batch-level draft-token selection (TETRIS); lossless compression of TP collectives (ZipCCL, LEXI, TensorRT-LLM PCIe low-precision AR); lossless FP8/bf16 weight compression for decode (DFloat11, ECF8, ANS tile-level 2606.15789, ZipServ); spec decoding for hybrid/linear-attention models (STree, SpecLA, TreeWY); MTP head improvement (FastMTP 2509.18362, LK losses 2602.23881, Qwen3.5 EAGLE-3 vs MTP model card); nano-batch intra-device overlap (NanoFlow). Sources read: abstracts plus the relevant method/result sections (HTML) of Seesaw, TETRIS, ECF8, FastMTP, LK losses, and the ANS paper abstract.

## Candidates, ranked by my judgment (payoff if it works vs T31-003)

### 1. Mixed-step FP8 all-gather of the out_proj input + duplicated out_proj weights (deck card 10, adapted). Payoff if it works ~+2-3%; odds ~40%
- Condition: comm bytes per token > the cost of recomputing. Measured here: the mixer AR is the largest exposed item in mixed steps (T30-002). AG bytes 3072 x 1.03 B vs AR 5120 x 2 B per token: -69%.
- Costs: 2x out_proj FLOPs (~8% of layer GEMM FLOPs); +0.98 GiB/rank of FP8 weights (needs KV 4.3 + two-slot GDN, KB82). Needs a ruling: FP8 activation bytes on the link (they are bitwise the bytes the W8A8 GEMM consumes).
- Arithmetic: mixer AR ~5% of wall x 69% = -3.3%, +1.6% from the extra GEMM: net ~+1.7% from mixed steps. Decode stays on the AR path; or with the AG at decode, -1.6 ms AR, +1 ms HBM, minus the lost prefetch window: ~0-1.5%.

### 2. Lossless FP8 weight coding in the e4m3 decode GEMM, aimed at gate_up (ECF8 / ANS tile-level / ZipServ). Payoff if it works ~+1.5-2.5%; odds ~20%
- Condition: decode GEMMs HBM-bound on weight bytes, and ALU room to decode in the loop. Here: FP8 weights 6.55 bits/byte order-0 entropy (KB47; ECF8 reports 10-15% on FP8 checkpoints, e.g. Qwen3-8B-FP8 -9.8%, which agrees). gate_up (89 MB) is the T31 wall: a cold stream and MMA that don't overlap, kept on Marlin (KB98). The register-direct e4m3 kernel has ALU slack that Marlin lacked (KB49 negative was on Marlin). A fixed-rate code (PFOR ~0.905) is decodable in registers; Huffman/ANS is not.
- Arithmetic: gate_up 85 -> ~75 us x 64 = -0.64 ms/step plus a share of down cold: ~-0.8 ms = 3.3% of decode, ~+2% e2e. The published gains are capacity-driven (bigger batches), not bandwidth: ECF8 Table 2, and the ANS paper's +1.2-1.6x comes from batch size. Our batch is fixed at 16, so only the bandwidth part transfers.

### 3. Batch-level draft-token budgeting (TETRIS, ACL 2025; same family as SmartSpec/DSD). Payoff if it works ~+1-2%; odds ~30%
- Condition: drafts with uneven confidence across the batch, and a fixed verify budget. Measured here: budgeted K_MAX=3 at fixed M=48 raises E +5.0% (2.468 vs 2.350) for +0.8 ms/step (KB85, job 174); 47% of step-1 drafts have conf >= 0.95 (KB60). The -2.6% e2e loss sat in adaptive plumbing outside the step (T27-003).
- Ceiling with perfect plumbing: E +5%, step +3.3% = ~+1.6% of decode, ~+1-1.5% e2e. TETRIS itself reports +2-5% over standard SD on L40/H100 at batch 64.

### 4. Tighter lossless AR coding (exponent remap/Huffman: ZipCCL top-7 exponents >97% -> 3 bits; LEXI exponent entropy < 3 bits). Payoff if it works <= ~+1%
- Today's codec is 0.781 (KB43); exponent-only Huffman gives ~0.69-0.70: -11% bytes. Decode AR transport gains are mostly given back through the prefetch coupling (KB78). The mixed-step DMA lane is raw because encode/decode on SMs didn't pay (T1-031). At most ~+1%.

### 5. Better MTP head (FastMTP self-distilled recursive head; LK acceptance losses). Payoff if it works ~0-1.5%
- FastMTP's post-training per-step acceptance (80/56/36% on MiMo-7B) equals what our native head already does (78/56, KB60). LK losses give only +0.8% acceptance length for DeepSeek MTP at T=0 (+5.6% at T=1); we run T=0. T3's fine-tunes measured within +-0.25 pt (KB60). The Qwen3.5 EAGLE-3 card shows native MTP has the higher acceptance length (3.28 vs 3.03 on MT-Bench). Nothing here beats our head.

### Considered and rejected (condition absent here)
- **Seesaw** (PP prefill / TP decode, MLSys 2025, 1.36x avg on PCIe A10/L4): it re-shards by reloading shards from CPU memory and batches prefills until a CPU KV buffer fills. It is offline-only, with no TTFT measurement. Here: closed loop c=16 with a TTFT p99 gate; ~6.75 GB/rank per switch at ~24 GB/s = ~0.3 s; keeping both layouts needs ~6.75 GB/rank against 1-1.5 GiB of slack. Not transferable.
- **NanoFlow** (nano-batch overlap, OSDI 2025): its premise is a compute-bound workload. Decode here is HBM-bound, and comm/GEMM co-run is zero-sum (KB59/62/70). The mixed-step 2-wave version was measured by T30: ceiling ~+2%, host-bound in server (KB100).
- **Tree speculation for hybrids (STree, NeurIPS 2025; SpecLA; TreeWY):** at our per-sequence budget of 3 rows, a chain gives E = 1 + 0.78 + 0.56 = 2.34. A top-2 tree at position 1 gives ~1 + 0.88 = 1.88. Larger trees add rows, and extra rows cost AR + GEMM + drafter (KB95: k=3 capped at ~+2.4% e2e).
- **TensorRT-LLM PCIe low-precision AllReduce:** lossy (FP8 on the wire); forbidden.
- **Peer-to-peer on 4090s via a patched open kernel driver:** would attack the largest bucket, but needs a driver install / root; forbidden.
- **MPK megakernel, TokenWeave:** costed in T32-1 (<= +3-5% and <= +2.5%).

## Conclusion and what I would propose if the run resumed
- No single candidate, from the deck or from my research, credibly reaches +5% on this system. Twenty-odd teams have pushed every compute component to a measured floor. The one large non-floor bucket, PCIe AR at ~15-17% of wall, is locked by the hardware (no P2P), by rules (lossless comm), and by the measured zero-sum overlap.
- The best package I could defend is #1 + #2 + #3. They hit different buckets (mixed-step mixer AR, decode gate_up bytes, decode tokens per step), so they mostly stack: payoff if all work ~+4.5-7%, central ~+2.5%, chance of reaching +5% about 15-20%. Three independent builds; ~100-140 GPU-min.
- If funded, order: (a) seed microbench for #1 (AG fp8 + full-K out_proj vs AR at R = 128/256/512/1024, plus a memory check at KV 4.3 with two-slot GDN), after a manager ruling on FP8 bytes on the link; (b) a fixed-rate weight code in the T31 e4m3 kernel on gate_up, cold-flushed standalone (kill if gate_up isn't >= 8% faster); (c) TETRIS-style budgeting only if (a) or (b) lands, since its plumbing cost is the known risk.
- Honest alternative: close the external slot and spend the pool on exploit-sized stacking of these 1-3% items.
