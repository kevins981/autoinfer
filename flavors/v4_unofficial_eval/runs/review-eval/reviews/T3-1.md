1. **K2-a: the measured overhead is real, but the claimed critical-path saving is not.**  
   [6-ranking.md:9](/scratch/kevinsong/learning/autoinfer/flavors/v4_unofficial_eval/runs/review-eval/reports/T3-sweep/6-ranking.md:9) says “single stream: yes, nothing runs beside it.” The trace contradicts this:

   | Kernel | Calls/step | Mean duration |
   |---|---:|---:|
   | Kernel2, grid `4×1×27` | 48 | 7.04 µs |
   | splitKreduce, grid `2×3×1` | 48 | 18.12 µs |

   Together they total **1.208 ms/step**, confirming your attribution. However, the reduction overlaps qkvz in **11,999 of 12,000 instances**, and its tail beyond qkvz is **zero in every instance**. The ba kernel starts about 3.11 µs before qkvz on average.

   One reproducible example, GPU 0, timestamps in ns:

   ```

   splitKreduce    stream 17    1308765059–1308771875
   ```

   A better ba kernel could reduce contention or the small leading interval, but **1 ms of decode saving is unsupported**. Demote #1 pending a graph-replay benchmark of the **whole projection prologue**, including concurrent qkvz. An isolated F.linear comparison cannot establish the claimed benefit. Also, at M=N=48, input reads add another 0.492 MB alongside the 0.492 MB weights.
