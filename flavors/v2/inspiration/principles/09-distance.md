# Distance

**The principle.** Work needs its inputs at the place where it happens. Moving things takes time in proportion to how much is moved and how far, and the paths have limited capacity. Time spent moving is not time spent working. How much movement there is depends on where things are kept and where the work is done, and both are choices.

**The waste.** Movement that a different placement would avoid.

**General directions.**
- **Bring the work to the data:** instead of the data to the work.
- **Keep together what is used together:** so one trip serves many needs.
- **Move less:** only the part that is needed, or a smaller form of it.
- **Move once:** not the same thing back and forth.
- **Keep the most used nearest:** the nearest places are few; give them to what is needed most often.

**Illustrations** (examples only; the best use of this principle may look like none of them). A workshop keeps its most used tools within arm's reach. A retailer puts warehouses near its customers. A question is sent to the database instead of fetching the whole table to answer it.
