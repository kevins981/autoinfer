# Predictability

**The principle.** Waiting for a result is necessary only if the result can't be predicted. If a guess is usually right, and checking a guess costs less than waiting for the answer, work can go ahead on the guess and be corrected in the rare cases it was wrong. The gain is roughly (chance of being right x time saved) - (chance of being wrong x work wasted). It needs three things: a cheap guess, a cheap check, and a way to undo.

**The waste.** Time spent waiting for an answer that was largely known in advance.

**General directions.**
- **Guess and verify:** proceed on the likely answer; check it when the real one arrives.
- **Prepare ahead:** get ready what is likely to be needed next.
- **Follow several possibilities:** and keep the one that turns out right.
- **Improve the odds or lower the price:** a better guess, a cheaper check, or a cheaper undo.

**Illustrations** (examples only; the best use of this principle may look like none of them). A processor guesses which way a branch will go and runs ahead. A waiter starts a regular's usual order when they walk in. A disk reads the next blocks before they are asked for.
