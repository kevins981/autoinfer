# T38-1 (moonshot, leader-38): the all-reduce stops being a barrier — stream it into the consumer GEMM

## The idea
In the champion's decode step every layer runs `GEMM -> all-reduce (host-staged, PCIe) -> add+RMSNorm -> next GEMM`, strictly in sequence.
The next GEMM (gate_up after the mixer's out_proj AR; the next layer's in_proj / qkv after the MLP's down AR) is
weight-streaming and DRAM-bound at 64 rows, and **its weight stream does not depend on the all-reduce at all**. Only its
activation K-chunks do, and those depend on the reduced row column by column: h = bf16(residual + bf16(p0+p1)),
GEMM input bf16(h*g), and RMSNorm's per-row 1/rms can move into the GEMM's fp32 epilogue (standing manager ruling of
2026-11-09, "moving RMSNorm's per-row 1/rms past the following linear").
So I build a dataflow pair:
- an AR kernel that delivers the reduced row in **column chunks** (all rows x ~640 cols): per chunk it adds the peer's
  partial, writes the new residual and bf16(h*g), accumulates per-row sum of squares, and raises a device flag;
- a weight-only FP8 GEMM (bf16 activations, same weights/layout as today) launched **concurrently**, each CTA walking all
  of K for its N-slice in order: it streams weights immediately and waits on chunk c's flag only before loading
  activation chunk c; inv_rms applied per row in the epilogue (fp32), as the ruling describes.
**Assumption rejected:** "an all-reduce is a barrier: the next layer's GEMM starts only when the complete reduced,
normalized row exists." The current design hides AR time only by L2-prefetching the next weights during the AR
(the hint prefetch lands ~40% per the champion's own code comments; gate_up after the AR still takes 95 us for 89 MB,
i.e. plain DRAM speed), so the AR's ~50 us x 2 per layer stays on the critical path.

- job 383: FP8 mma peak 330 TFLOPS (f32 acc); job 386: f16-accumulate route dead (overflows) — not part of this proposal.

## Upside (if it fully works)
Per layer: AR1 (50 us) hidden behind gate_up (95 us): exposed ~= first-chunk latency ~10 us -> saves ~40 us.
AR2 (50 us) overlapped with in_proj/qkv (~45 us cold): ends ~one chunk after the AR (~58 us vs 84 today) -> saves ~25 us.
=> ~45-65 us/layer x 64 = **2.9-4.1 ms of a ~26 ms decode step (11-16%)**. Decode-only steps are ~70% of the benchmark
(job 382: decode 576 steps 13.5 s vs mixed 63 steps ~6.2 s of 21.7 s) => **+8% to +12% over the champion**,
before extending the same dataflow to the drafter's ~13 ARs/step and to mixed steps (more upside, not counted).
Bound-checked against physics: the AR bytes (~500 KB compressed per AR) need ~20 us per PCIe hop; the overlapped step
still exceeds the DRAM floor (~15 ms), so the claim is "move ~3-4 ms of the 7 ms AR off the critical path", not more.

## Riskiest assumption and cheapest test (milestone 1)
Riskiest: that the **dependent** overlap keeps the independent overlap's numbers: a GEMM that waits on per-chunk flags
(ordered K walk, N-split only, no Marlin K-striping) still runs at DRAM speed at 64 rows, and the chunked AR's first-chunk
latency + final inv_rms wait stay ~10 us. Test (standalone, 2 processes, real FP8 weights in the champion's Marlin layout,
real shapes): [chunked AR + combine + flags] || [flag-waiting GEMM with inv_rms epilogue] vs the champion's sequence
[hostar(with its L2 prefetch) -> fused add+RMSNorm -> Marlin]. Pass: >= 35 us saved per gate_up pair and >= 15 us per
in_proj pair, output within bf16 rounding of the stock path (max-abs/rel diff reported). ~5-10 GPU-min.
