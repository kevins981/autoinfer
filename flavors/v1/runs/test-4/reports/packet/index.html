What worked

  • Host-staged all-reduce for small (decode/verify) messages: 68 → 45 µs at 48 tokens, bitwise equal to NCCL, +5.7%.
  • Cheaper drafting and sampling with existing vLLM flags (FP8 draft lm_head, local-argmax drafting, batch-sharded sampling): acceptance unchanged, +2.8%.
  • The two stack nearly additively: +8.7% together.
  • Order-controlled A/Bs (ABAB) exposed a ~2% server-drift artifact before a false promotion.

What didn't

  • Draft depth 3: acceptance rose from 2.35 to 2.76, but throughput was flat or worse and TTFT p99 failed the gate, twice.
  • NCCL env tuning (protocol, channels): no gain at any message size.
  • More all-reduce kernel tuning: the link isn't full-duplex for SM zero-copy, and the kernel is already near the floor.
  • Prefill all-reduce overlap: works mechanically, but the gain can't be measured at c=16.
  • Custom exact greedy target argmax: equal to the existing batch-sharded sampling.

Open questions

  • One-graph capture of the spec-decode glue (drafter passes + sampling + attention-metadata builds): ~1.6 ms/step of real CPU idle, an estimated +3-4%, but invasive. The largest measured lever left on decode.
  • Marlin is ~51% of GPU time. It measured near its own floor at M=48, but tile and split-K changes were never tried (see Untried ideas).
  • Fusing residual-add + RMSNorm into the host all-reduce (~1.6 ms/step of norms and similar): not tried.
  • Why depth 3 blows up TTFT p99: suspected queueing behind 2048-token prefill chunks; not established.
  • Hardening: the host all-reduce assumes both ranks construct it (if only one fails, the ranks disagree on the backend). T1-004's code is hack-grade.
