# T44-1: the link is full duplex if the writers stop flooding it. Rate-matched all-reduce for mixed steps

Team T44 (research), leader-44. Evidence: bank/T44-001 (jobs 397, 398; 1.0 GPU-min). Sparring: sparring/ T44-1.

## The finding (already measured)
The run has treated SM-driven host traffic as half duplex for 40 teams: KB5, KB73 ("SM posted writes break duplex"), and
KB109 ("kernel access to host-mapped memory moves ~22 GB/s per GPU whatever the direction mix"). Cut-through and
overlap designs were closed on that basis (T36-002, the T15/T16 hybrids).

T44-001 shows the cause is **oversubscription, not the SM path**:
- No store variant (cs/wb/wt/volatile, 4/8/16 B, per-warp 4 KB runs), host memory type (WC, THP, cudaHostAlloc), LLC
  placement or link power state changes anything.
- What changes everything is the writers' offered rate. Above the upstream link rate (~26 GB/s), reads, from SMs or from
  the copy engine, queue behind the posted-write backlog and starve. That is consistent with PCIe ordering (reads don't pass
  posted writes). We did not confirm it with counters.
- Held to ~20-23 GB/s, either by pacing or by ~1:2 writer:reader CTAs, SM write + SM read reaches the copy-engine duplex
  reference at >=1.5 MB:
  - 12 MB: 678 -> 568 us (CE+CE 562);
  - SM write + CE-H2D: 915 -> 576 (fully serialized before).
- At decode size (384 KB) nothing gains: that AR is latency/ramp-bound.

## Why it matters here (code read of the champion T37-002)
- **hostar** (host_staged_ar.cu): writer and reader blocks run concurrently with per-chunk flags, at a 1:1 ratio:
  - 8+8 blocks at <=600 KB;
  - 16+16 at <=8 MB;
  - 32+32 beyond.
  In the E sweep, 1:1 is in the oversubscribed region (12 MB: nw32/nr32 743, nw64/nr64 677 vs best 568), so the
  write and read phases mostly serialize.
- **Token-split mixed steps** run the DMA lane (CE D2H + SM read) and the hostar lane (SM writes) at the same time.
  The unpaced hostar writers are exactly the case that serialized CE-H2D and SM-R in T44-001.
  - KB66: both chunks' ARs run concurrently with no compute beside them, 15-23% of those steps' span.
  - KB100/KB104: the "~631 us duplex floor" at 1100 rows and the latency-bound mixed AR were measured on this
    configuration.
- **Mixed-step exposed AR is the largest non-floor cost left in the trial.** KB77 puts exposed comm at 6.8 s/trial for
  R<256; KB91 at +5.2 ms (small) / +9.9 ms (mid) per mixed step. Prefill AR transport is ~8 s of an ~80 s trial
  (0.72 us/token/AR).

## What could change for future teams
- **"SM host traffic is half duplex" is false**, and so is the conclusion built on it, that cut-through/overlapped AR
  transports can't pay. Any AR or all-gather design on this machine should budget the link as ~21-22 GB/s *each way at
  once*, provided writers are rate-matched.
- **A rule for co-scheduling link users:** the sum of posted-write rates must stay below link rate, or every concurrent
  reader stalls, including the copy engines. This also explains why some overlap experiments "interfered" for no clear
  reason (e.g. KB65/KB67's variable CE rates beside SM traffic). That reading is a hypothesis to check.

## Forecast (honest)
- **Ceiling:** if the mixed-step AR's write and read phases overlap fully, a large AR goes from ~W+R toward max(W,R)+ramp.
  At 12 MB that is 678 -> ~570 in isolation (-16%); for the two-lane token split, up to ~-35% on the concurrent phase.
- **Exposed mixed-step AR ~8-10% of the trial:**
  - -15% -> **~+1.2-1.5% e2e**;
  - -25% -> ~+2-2.5%.
- **Decode:** nothing expected (384 KB is latency-bound; KB78's prefetch coupling).
- **Odds:** >= +1% e2e ~50%; >= +3% ~10%.
- Main risk (spar T44-1, turn 4): faster transfer may shorten time that is already hidden, not the exposed critical
  path, especially with the concurrent DMA lane. Stage 1 measures exactly that: exposed span of the concurrent lanes,
  not isolated transfer time. The upper forecast needs >16% off the exposed part.
- This is an exploit-sized speedup on top of the research result. The finding stands either way and is already filed.
