# T38-3: Pipeline-parallel engine with dynamic draft trees (no all-reduce)

Team T38 (moonshot), leader-38. Evidence: bank/T38-001 (champion profile), T38-002 (tree acceptance), T38-003 (level-wise
drafting + PP-shape GEMMs, job 390); my nsys profile of the champion (agents/leader-38/prof_champ_node/, job 383).

## The idea, and the assumption it rejects
The champion is TP=2: every layer is split, so every token row crosses the host-staged all-reduce 141 times per step.
**hostar_kernel is 20.4% of all GPU kernel time** in my profile of the champion's serving run (measured). The AR is at the PCIe
limit and other teams have shown it can't be hidden under GEMMs. The design assumption I reject: **"the two GPUs must
cooperate on every layer"**. In a 2-stage pipeline, each GPU owns whole layers, and per microbatch only one ~1 MB activation
crosses between GPUs. The price: to keep both GPUs busy, two microbatches are in flight, so every weight byte is read twice per
round. Draft trees are what fill the extra rows: the stock MTP head gives 4.1-4.4 tokens/step with level-wise trees
(T38-003) against 2.73 for today's chain.

## What the measurements say (honest version)
- **Decode, measured inputs (T38-003):** N=15 nodes, D=6 levels, K=4, 8 seqs x 16 rows = M=128 per microbatch. Period
  ~43.8 ms = GEMM 32.6 (measured) + non-GEMM 3 (assumed) + full-vocab head 1.6 (estimated) + drafting 6.3 (estimated:
  6 levels x MTP layer + 98K draft head per microbatch) + transfers 0.3. Gives 16 x 4.09 tokens per 43.8 ms = ~1490 tok/s,
  vs the champion's ~1747 (16 x 2.73 / 25 ms measured): **decode about -15% with today's kernels.**
- **Decode, if the recoverable parts are recovered:**
  - narrow-K GEMM configs (K=6144 and 17408 shapes run at 1.5x floor at M=128 today, while wide-N shapes run at
    1.07x): 32.6 -> ~28.7;
  - a smaller draft head, 0.6 ms/level: 6.3 -> 3.6;
  - together: period ~37 ms, **decode ~-2..+1%.**
- **Prefill (where the gain is):** a 60-token mixed step costs 311 us/token more than a decode step in the champion
  (measured): GEMM 113, AR 93, chunked GDN 30, misc 42. PP has no AR. The PP-shape GEMM slope is 47-103 us/row/stage
  (measured, job 390), with two stages on two GPUs. So prefill per token **~30-40% cheaper** (estimated; bubbles are
  avoidable by splitting consecutive chunks of one prompt across microbatches A then B, which pipeline order permits).
- **Wall model:** t_new/t_old = 0.76/r + 0.24 x (1 - s), where r = decode throughput ratio and s = prefill saving. Prefill
  is ~24% of wall time (from T37-001's cost model, pointed to me).
  - At s = 0.40, +5% overall needs r >= 0.94.
  - Fully works (r ~1.0, s ~0.4): **~+11% over the champion.**
  - Today's kernels (r = 0.85): -2%.

## Riskiest assumption and cheapest test (stage 1, ~12 GPU-min)
**Riskiest:** PP actually cuts the marginal cost of prompt tokens by >= 30% in mixed serving (not just in prefill-only
throughput), including weight-pass amortization and scheduling.

**Test:** the champion's engine with speculation off, PP=2 vs TP=2, same kernels, the benchmark's load, both nsys-profiled.
Regress step time on prefill tokens per step to get the marginal cost per prompt token in each configuration. This also gives
in-server PP decode step times, to validate the 3 ms non-GEMM assumption.
- **Pass:** PP marginal prefill cost <= 0.70x TP's, and in-server PP decode per-stage time within 10% of the T38-003 model.
- **Fail:** close the direction and write it up.
- A known risk is that the champion's code may need small fixes to run at PP=2 (custom kernels at full width; vLLM disallows
  async scheduling with PP).

## Roadmap
1. **Stage 1 (above), ~12 GPU-min.** Pass criteria as above.
2. **Stage 2, decode recovery, ~15 GPU-min:**
   - narrow-K W8A8 configs at M=96-160 (microbench);
   - a cheaper draft head, with acceptance measured offline (CPU) and timing on GPU;
   - the exact tree GDN kernel (ancestor-masked chunked delta rule + accepted-path commit), checked for correctness against
     sequential recursion, and timed.

   Must show predicted r >= 0.94 from measured parts.
3. **Stage 3, build, ~80-120 GPU-min:**
   - PP=2 serving with an uneven layer split (head and drafter on GPU1);
   - level-wise tree drafting with MTP;
   - tree verification (attention tree mask, tree GDN, tree conv);
   - microbatch-balanced chunked prefill;
   - CUDA graphs per tree size.

   Then an unofficial abba vs the champion, followed by the official eval.

## Forecast
- **Measured:** the AR share (20.4%), tree acceptance (T38-002/003), PP GEMM costs (job 390), the champion's decode
  step (25 ms) and the mixed-step diff.
- **Assumed:** prefill share 24% (T37-001), PP non-GEMM 3 ms, drafter per-level cost, recoverability of GEMM and draft
  head, no scheduling bubbles, async-scheduling loss.
- **Upside if it fully works: ~+11%** (decode ~even, prefill ~40% cheaper).
- **Central estimate: ~0 to +2%. Odds of >= +5% official: ~10-15%.** The engineering is very large (it rewrites
  parallelism, verification and drafting at once), and decode must land within 6% of the champion.
- **GPU:** stage 1 ~12 GPU-min, which T38's balance covers (40.8 left); whole roadmap ~110-150 GPU-min.
