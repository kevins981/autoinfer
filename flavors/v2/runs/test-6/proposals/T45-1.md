# T45-1 (research, leader-45): every row costs PCIe. A marginal-row rule for this machine, and the one bet it leaves

## Summary
My search found no new mechanism that survives napkin math. It did find a rule that explains why so many
tokens-per-pass ideas in this run came out flat (trees, deeper k, small prefill budgets, piggyback). The rule leaves one
bet whose physics argument contradicts two KB entries (KB64, KB108). I ask for no new grant: stage 1 fits in the
20 GPU-min seed (<= 6 GPU-min).

## 1. The idea: rows are priced by the link, not by the GPU
Observed, from records:
- PCIe AR transport costs about 0.07 ms per row per pass, at every size and with every transport.
  - Hostar with the codec: 620 us at 1100 rows. The duplex CE floor is 631 us (KB104).
  - That is 0.56-0.64 us per row per AR, times 128 ARs.
  - At decode sizes, the bytes part of the AR is the same per row (KB109). Duplex adds nothing below 1.5 MB (KB115).
- One accepted token is worth ~0.61 ms of pass time (26.2 ms / 42.7 tokens; KB111).

Interpretation (the rule):
- An extra row pays only if it brings at least (0.07 + GEMM band cost + its share of draft cost) / 0.61 tokens.
- That is ≥ 0.14 tokens per row when GEMMs and drafting are free, and ~0.2 or more with today's draft levels.
- Draft rows are probabilistic tokens, and their marginal yield falls as the tree or chain grows.
  - T38's best-first tree adds ~0.21 tokens per row going from 4 to 11 nodes, but needs ~6 draft levels (+2.5 ms).
  - In my closed-loop simulator (T45-002), an 11-node tree is -0.1% at a row cost of 0.08 with 6 draft levels. It is
    +5.2% only if drafting stayed at 3 levels.
- **Prompt rows are the one row type whose yield is certain.** A prompt row placed in a pass that is below the tensor
  ridge saves its whole compute (~0.093 ms per token per GPU at the FP8 roof). It still pays only the link (~0.07).
  - In a prefill event the same token costs 0.166 ms, because compute and AR run one after the other (KB111).
  - The average prompt load is ~45 rows per pass: 104K prompt tokens over ~2300 passes.
  - That is about the realistic tensor slack of a 64-row decode pass: ~30-80 rows, depending on MMA efficiency and
    the Marlin gate_up at half tensor rate.

What follows for future teams:
- Do not buy draft rows on this machine without cutting the cost per draft level first. This explains T38's flat
  trees and T35/KB63/KB95's flat deeper k.
- The tokens-per-pass budget should go to prompt rows below the ridge, in passes that cost no more than a decode
  pass. Two runs of the run's evidence do not refute this, because neither tested that joint condition:
  - small budgets on eager mixed steps: -19% / -2.6% (KB108);
  - graphed GDN core with the 2048 budget: +0.29% (KB107).
- **Contrarian claim (falsifiable):** KB64's "piggyback can't pay (0.36 ms/row)" is an artifact of the following, not
  physics:
  - ~7-15 ms of fixed per-step overhead on eager mixed steps;
  - GEMM kernels that sit at 1.1-1.5x floor in the M=64-128 band (T38 pp_gemm, KB64/KB76).

## 2. Why it is novel, and what is not
- The mechanism, prompt chunks riding in memory-bound decode passes, is SARATHI-style chunked prefill. That is known.
- New relative to the run:
  - (a) the link-priced row rule, which unifies trees, k, budgets and piggyback under one number;
  - (b) the derivation that the average prompt load fits inside the ridge slack;
  - (c) the claim that the run's two negatives on small budgets and piggyback are artifacts of eager mixed steps and
    M-band GEMMs, together with the measurement that would refute it.
- Supporting kills, all at 0 GPU-min, are in T45-002:
  - CPU/AMX prefill;
  - alternative TP partitions (min-cut);
  - async lane;
  - drafter-phase co-scheduling;
  - context AR codec;
  - lossy self-draft rows;
  - PP decode with prefill in the bubble;
  - coalescing.

## 3. First step (stage 1, <= 6 GPU-min from the seed; one worker)
- **Microbench.** Use T35's standalone 64-layer champion-kernel chain (agents/leader-35.w1/ms1: real t31 e4m3 GEMMs
  and Marlin gate_up, CUDA graphs). Rerun it at M = 64, 80, 96, 112, 128 in TP shapes, cold weights, with the
  champion's dispatch per M.
- **Readout.** Marginal GEMM cost per row between 64 and 112, in us per row per pass (all 64 layers). Write the
  estimate next to the measurement: the floor argument says < 20 us per row up to ~100 rows.
- **Kill rule.**
  - If the marginal cost is > 40 us per row, sub-ridge prompt rows cost ≥ 0.11 ms with AR. The saving over the
    0.166 event cost is then < 35%, worth ≤ ~+3% e2e after glue.
  - In that case I close T45, and the row rule stands as the deliverable.
- **Pass.** If the marginal cost is ≤ 40 us per row, stage 2 builds a static [decode rows + fixed prompt chunk] pass,
  graphed and including the GDN chunk core (T32_GDN_CAP asset), and runs a same-job ABBA.
  - That is exploit-sized (est. 40-80 GPU-min). I would propose it as a hand-off rather than run it under a research
    grant.

## Stage 1 result (bank/T45-003, job 408, 0.8 GPU-min): KILL rule hit
- Measured: marginal GEMM cost from 64 to 96 rows is 86 us/row/pass (rule: ≤40; estimate beforehand: <20).
  - It is a step at 64->80 (+145 us/row), then 27 us/row from 80 to 96.
  - gate_up is 55 of the 86.
- Interpretation: Marlin's bf16-MMA gate_up hits its compute roof at ~M=75 (117 us at peak vs 131.5 measured at
  M=96). The sub-ridge slack I assumed at the FP8 rate does not exist for the largest GEMM.
- A prompt row in a decode pass would cost ~0.156 ms (GEMM + AR) vs 0.166 in an event. KB64/KB108 are physics given
  today's gate_up, not artifacts.
- The rule stands: rows are priced by link + GEMM band, ~0.16 ms above 64 rows.
- Only opening: an e4m3-MMA gate_up holding ~95 us at M=96-128 (untested), plus a t31 path for M>96. A stage 2 would
  have to measure exactly that, in one standalone kernel bench, before anything else.
